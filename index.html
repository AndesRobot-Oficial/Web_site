11th

RoboCup 2025 - Salvador, Brazil

In our first official RoboCup participation, AndesRobot Team finished in 11th place. The experience in Salvador gave us a clear picture of where our robot stands and which capabilities we need to strengthen.

For RoboCup 2027 we are reorganizing the team around defined research lines, with measurable goals for each competition task.

Research Lines

Our work toward RoboCup 2027 is organized in six research lines. Each one addresses open problems in service robotics and is linked to specific tasks of the RoboCup@Home rulebook.

Semantic Mapping and Navigation

Maps that store both the geometry of a house and its meaning: rooms, furniture and objects. We also study navigation that respects people's personal space, using ROS 2 and Nav2.

Related tasks: Carry My Luggage, GPSR

Open-Vocabulary Object Perception

Recognition and segmentation of objects that were not in the training set, using vision-language models and RGB-D data, with the constraint of running on the robot's onboard GPU in real time.

Related tasks: Storing Groceries, Serve Breakfast

Mobile Manipulation

Grasp planning for household objects with different shapes and materials, and coordination between the mobile base and the arm for pick-and-place in cluttered scenes.

Related tasks: Clean the Table, Storing Groceries

Human-Robot Interaction and Dialogue

Speech recognition in noisy environments and dialogue with language models. This line builds on our previous work on adapting the robot's language to the user's personality.

Related tasks: Receptionist, GPSR

Task Planning from Natural Language

Converting spoken commands into executable plans, combining language models with behavior trees and checking each step against what the robot can actually do.

Related tasks: GPSR, Serve Breakfast

Person Detection and Tracking

Detecting, identifying and following people even after occlusions, and recognizing gestures and poses to support natural interaction.

Related tasks: Carry My Luggage, Receptionist

How we work

  1. 1. SimulationEach module is developed and tested first in Gazebo.
  2. 2. EvaluationWe measure performance using the RoboCup@Home scoring criteria.
  3. 3. IntegrationValidated modules are integrated on the real robot with ROS 2.
  4. 4. PublicationResults are documented and the code is shared openly.
